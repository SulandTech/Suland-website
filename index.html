HOW IT WORKS

AI proposes. Physics checks.
You decide.

Designing a robot cell can take weeks of layouts, reach checks, cycle-time estimates and trials before a quote goes out.

SULAND Studio is not a simulator you program by hand: you describe the cell, the AI proposes it, physics checks it.

The first pass takes minutes: compare options before buying any hardware, and quote with confidence, every figure measured in simulation.

01 / DESCRIBE

Say what the customer needs

Describe the cell in a sentence: the parts, the totes, the cycle time. Choose the robot, its tool, the camera and the layout yourself, or take Studio's suggestions, step by step.

02 / APPROVE

Nothing changes until you accept

Every proposal is shown first, in words, and a layout change is outlined in 3D. You accept it, change it or turn it down.

03 / VALIDATE

Measured in physics simulation

The digital twin runs the whole task. Cycle time, parts placed and motion are measured against the requirements.

04 / DEPLOY

From the digital twin to the real cell

A proposal for the customer and a list of what the real cell still needs, both written from the validated run.
Coming next: the control software validated on the twin, deployed to the real cell.

PLATFORM CAPABILITIES

Four capabilities,
one workspace.

Every picture below comes from the prototype, running in simulation.

01 / PLATFORM

SULAND Studio

One workspace built for the whole job: describe the cell, build its digital twin, validate it, deploy it.

02 / INTELLIGENCE

AI-Powered Engineering Automation

Say what you need in plain language. A local AI model turns it into concrete changes to the cell: robot, tool, layout, camera, requirements. Each change is checked against the components' limits, such as reach, camera height and field of view. Cloud models, used only on demand, are planned.

03 / SIMULATION

Robotics & Simulation

Compare robots and layouts on the same task in physics simulation, with cycle time, parts placed and motion measured, before any hardware is chosen.

04 / PERCEPTION

Perception & Edge AI

A simulated camera feeds the robot's perception while the cell is validated. Running the same perception on edge computers, such as NVIDIA Jetson, is on the roadmap.

DEVELOPMENT ROADMAP

From one pick cell
to any robot cell.

The prototype shows the method on a simple pick-and-place cell. The goal is an AI that understands how the components of a robot cell connect. Describe any cell, and SULAND Studio composes it from the robots, tools, sensors, conveyors and controllers it needs, checks every connection and interface, validates it in simulation and deploys it. Milestones may evolve as the product matures.
